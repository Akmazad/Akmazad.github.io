## The project: Oriental Braille

Blind and visually impaired learners in the Arabic-speaking world have limited access to affordable tactile
reading material, especially for Quranic and educational texts. The Oriental Braille Project
(مشروع برايل الشرقي) uses **artificial intelligence and 3D printing** to convert Arabic text into
**tactile cards with raised Braille**, so that readers can learn by touch.

The pipeline combines several parts of the lab's toolkit:

1. **Text processing.** Algorithms prepare Arabic text, including Quranic and educational passages, for
   conversion into Braille.
2. **Quantitative calibration.** Measurements set the dimensions of the cards and the raised dots so that the
   printed Braille is legible to touch.
3. **Statistical quality control.** Statistical methods assess conversion accuracy, error rate and the quality of
   the printed output.
4. **Mathematical optimisation.** Optimisation reduces production cost and material use, so the cards can be
   produced **affordably and locally**.

The goal is accessible, low-cost educational material that empowers people with visual disabilities and improves
their quality of life. It continues the lab's work with the King Salman Center for Disability Research
(see our [projects](/projects/)).
